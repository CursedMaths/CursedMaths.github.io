Blaschke products associated to ellipses: a simple characterization

Martin Baptiste, November 2026

For a finite Blaschke product \(B\), the solutions of \(B(z)=\lambda\) on the unit circle form a polygon. When do all these polygons circumscribe the same ellipse? The answer only depends on the critical values of \(B\).

The main result

Theorem. Let \(B\) be a Blaschke product of degree \(n\ge3\). Then \(B\) is associated to an ellipse if and only if

  1. \(B\) has exactly one critical value in the unit disk, or
  2. \(B\) has exactly two critical values in the unit disk, and all its critical points there are simple.

Equivalently, the monodromy group of \(B\) is a group of symmetries of the polygon: it is cyclic in case (a) and dihedral in case (b).

The proof is elementary. Follow the vertices of one polygon while \(\lambda\) travels around a critical value and comes back: the vertices come back permuted. The ellipse exists exactly when neighboring vertices always come back as neighbors, and a count of critical points shows that this happens exactly in cases (a) and (b).

Abstract

We study finite Blaschke products whose level sets on the unit circle are the vertices of polygons circumscribing a fixed ellipse (Poncelet polygons). Theorem X describes such a Blaschke product through the foci of a family of ellipses. Theorem Y gives the elliptic curve hidden behind the configuration. Theorem Z, the main new result, characterizes these Blaschke products through their critical values only. Behind it lies an isogeny between two elliptic curves, of which such a \(B\) is the shadow. A self-contained introduction to the objects involved (Riemann surfaces, elliptic functions, elliptic curves, isogenies) is included, written for readers who have never met them.
